## Design Context

### Users

The primary audience is prospective clients and collaborators looking for an experienced freelance software developer who can design and build polished web, mobile, and backend products. Visitors may arrive in Turkish or English, often with limited time to assess credibility, technical range, past work, and fit for a new project. The interface should help them quickly understand Erinç Polat's expertise, explore relevant case studies, and confidently start a conversation.

### Brand Personality

The brand is reliable, innovative, and modern. Its voice should be clear, capable, approachable, and grounded in real delivery experience rather than hype. The experience should create trust while communicating technological sophistication and an active interest in new product ideas.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use a clean, contemporary, light-first visual language with generous whitespace and strong hierarchy. Preserve the established palette: white backgrounds, warm near-black headings (`#342F2D`), restrained gray supporting text (`#8F9094`), and green (`#3CCF91`) as the primary accent and action color. Roboto should remain the readable body typeface, with Oswald used selectively for expressive section headings; Barlow Semi Condensed may support navigation where already established.

Favor well-composed project imagery, spacious layouts, softly rounded cards, subtle depth, and purposeful motion. Interactive and 3D elements should demonstrate technical capability without competing with the portfolio content or contact journey. Motion should feel smooth and controlled, and the bilingual Turkish/English experience should remain visually consistent. There are currently no specific reference sites or explicit visual anti-references; avoid generic technology-dashboard aesthetics, excessive decoration, gratuitous gradients, and effects that undermine credibility or readability.

The site should remain light themed unless a future requirement explicitly introduces dark mode. Provide a practical accessibility baseline: semantic structure, keyboard-operable controls, visible focus states, meaningful labels and alternative text, sufficient color contrast, readable text sizing, and respect for reduced-motion preferences. Accessibility improvements should feel native to the design rather than visually separate from it.

### Design Principles

1. **Earn trust quickly.** Make experience, services, project outcomes, and contact paths easy to understand and verify.
2. **Show innovation through execution.** Use refined interaction, motion, and technical demonstrations with purpose; avoid novelty that distracts from the work.
3. **Keep the visual system modern and disciplined.** Maintain generous spacing, clear typography, restrained color, consistent radii, and a strong content hierarchy.
4. **Let projects lead.** Give case-study imagery and outcomes visual priority, and keep supporting interface elements quiet enough to frame them well.
5. **Design inclusively by default.** Preserve usability across screen sizes, languages, keyboard input, assistive technology, and reduced-motion settings.
